Recommended experiments & hypotheses

  1. Mobile practice questions almost never lead to a submission device confidence medium
    A mobile-specific defect in the practice answer UI blocks or discourages submission. Candidates are the answer input or submit button being hidden by the on-screen keyboard, or a non-numeric keypad. Alternatively, the submit event does not fire on mobile, which would make this a tracking artefact rather than a behaviour gap. Either way, mobile practice users disengage before attempting.
    Intervention: Step 1: run a manual QA pass on real phones and confirm practice_question_submitted fires on mobile. Step 2: if a UI defect is confirmed, ship the minimal fix behind a mobile-only flag (e.g. inputmode=decimal on the answer field, a sticky or visible submit button) and compare mobile practice view→submit by variant.
    F-GA-devicegap-mobile-practice_view_to_submit F-GA-device-mobile F-GA-device-desktop F-GA-funnel-practice_view_to_submit F-GAP-practice_view_to_submit
  2. Forced non-interactive wait for the bot after a wrong/non-perfect submission pacing confidence low
    Players who finish early and face up to minutes of dead time leave before the result screen. This lowers battle completion and the chance they start another game.
    Intervention: Behind a feature flag, after the player's final submission in a bot battle, offer a 'See result now' action. It fast-forwards the bot's pre-sampled simulated finish and shows the result screen immediately.
    F-CODE-pacing.bot_wait.easy F-CODE-pacing.bot_wait.medium F-CODE-pacing.bot_wait.hard F-CODE-pacing.matchmaking F-GA-funnel-battle_finish F-GAP-noninteractive_wait_time F-GAP-opponent_type_mix
  3. Numeric questions without rounding/unit instructions mark correct answers wrong question_quality confidence low
    Players who answer these questions correctly at a different precision or unit (e.g. 0.57 instead of 0.5714, % instead of decimal, seconds instead of minutes) are marked wrong. This leads to frustration, disputes and abandonment on those questions.
    Intervention: Behind a flag, append a standardised format line to the flagged questions (e.g. 'Enter as a decimal rounded to 4 decimal places' or 'in years, to 2 d.p.'). Answers and tolerances stay unchanged.
    F-Q-ambiguous-format F-Q-summary F-GAP-question_solve_rate F-GAP-question_dispute_rate F-GAP-abandonment_by_difficulty F-GAP-ga4-dims
  4. Fixed-length Quant Battle phases make matches long pacing confidence low
    Players who finish blitz questions early wait out the clock. The long worst-case match length leads some to abandon mid-match or not start another battle.
    Intervention: Behind a flag, end the blitz phase early once the player has answered all available blitz items (or offer a 'Continue' button), leaving the tactical phase unchanged.
    F-CODE-pacing.quant_battle.easy F-CODE-pacing.quant_battle.medium F-CODE-pacing.quant_battle.hard F-CODE-matches-partial F-GAP-game_duration_distribution F-GA-funnel-battle_finish
  5. Every IB battle serves the identical question set retention confidence low
    Players who replay IB battles see the exact same questions. This removes novelty and challenge, so they are less likely to start another game or come back.
    Intervention: Behind a flag, expand each IB tier pool (e.g. by adding reviewed questions) so a match draws 3 from a larger set. Measure replay in the IB category.
